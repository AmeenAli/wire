WIRE: Weight Space Implicit Representation Encoding of Frozen Video LLMs

Fewer video tokens, plus a weight update generated at prefill. Prefill runs up to 4.1× faster, with higher accuracy than other efficient models.

  • ResultPrefill runs 3.4× faster at 256 frames and 4.1× at 1,024, and decoding keeps its speed. The first token arrives 7 to 13× faster than with other efficient video models.
  • WhyEach frame's 56 tokens shrink to 16, so the decoder reads a short input. A weight update built at prefill is merged before decoding.
  • FindingTrained with input dropout, WIRE encodes the video in its weights. With no visual tokens at all, the update adds 11.0 points on short Video-MME videos.

Overview · how it works

Read less, decode as fast as the backbone.

  • CompressA compressor that reads the question reduces each frame to 16 refined tokens. They take 16 of the frame's original positions, so the decoder still sees frame order and spacing.
  • GenerateHypernetworks at every fourth layer generate low-rank weight updates. They read the decoder's hidden states and the full-resolution tokens that compression removed.
  • MergeThe updates depend only on the video and question, so they are merged after prefill. Decoding then runs with no extra computation over the shorter cache.

01 · Results

Prefill runs up to 4.1× faster, and decoding is unchanged.

Sections 5.1 to 5.4 of the paper, with Figures 1 and 4, Tables 1, 2 and 4, and Appendices D and F.

The decoder of WIRE reads about 3× fewer positions than the uncompressed Qwen3-VL-8B, and prefill time falls with them. On short Video-MME videos, WIRE keeps 93.7% of the accuracy of the backbone. Decoding runs the unmodified backbone after the merge, and each generated token takes as long as before.

The weight update helps most where detail matters, such as counting.

Removing the update while keeping the compressed input isolates what it contributes. The Video-MME gain pools five checkpoints, and MVBench and LongVideoBench gain as well. Among task types, counting gains the most, with 3.36 points on Video-MME and 5.50 on MVBench.

With input dropout, the weights encode the video.

A variant with 6 tokens per frame is trained with input dropout, which removes all visual tokens from half of the training inputs. Its video can be identified from its update, and with no visual tokens the update adds 11.0 points.

02 · Use it

Run WIRE on your own videos.

Code by the authors. The recipe follows Section 3 of the paper.

The repository loads the four released models and holds their training and evaluation scripts. A CPU script rebuilds the paper's input lengths and parameter counts in a few seconds. The Colab tour shows the contraction on a sample clip and checks the merge, in about a minute.

GitHub: wire Open the Colab tour
  1. Compress. Pool each frame's visual tokens into a few queries and refine them with a bidirectional scan that reads the question at both ends.
  2. Contract. Before the first decoder layer, replace each frame's visual tokens with its refined tokens at original positions.
  3. Generate. Emit low-rank updates for the attention and feed-forward maps with hypernetworks that read the forward pass.
  4. Merge. Add the updates to the weights after prefill, decode with the unmodified backbone and subtract them before the next video.
  5. Train. Fit the compressor and the hypernetworks to match the same frozen model reading the full input.

03 · How it works

A few tokens per frame, and a weight update merged after prefill.

Section 3 of the paper, with Figure 2 and Appendix A.

A video language model places every frame in the decoder input as dozens of visual tokens. Each token passes through every layer, fills the cache and is attended by every generated token. WIRE keeps a few refined tokens per frame and adds weight updates whose cost does not grow with the answer.

Four steps from a long video to a short input.

Figure 2 of the paper (WIRE overview), animated with illustrative values.

Each step in one or two equations.

Decoding then runs the unmodified backbone over the contracted cache, and the updates are subtracted before the next video.
